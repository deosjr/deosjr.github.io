A browser with Wasm GC and tail call support is required for this demo. We recommend using either Mozilla Firefox or Google Chrome.

Sokoban

Put a level, the geometry, push, movement, win and projector pages and the wasd controls on the table. Then point the keyboard at the level using its whisker (the whiskers page hands them out) and push the crates onto the targets.

Move with w/a/s/d, undo with u and reset with r. Lifting a level off the table and putting it back also resets it, since its page code runs again from scratch. Once a level is solved it stops listening to keys altogether; lifting it off the table is the only way to play it again. Avoid q and e: they rotate the page under the mouse.

The pages are written in the spirit of PuzzleScript: a game is a handful of small rules about who wants to move where, plus an engine that turns those wants into movement. Sokoban's one rule, "if a player is trying to walk into a crate, try to push that crate", is the whole push page:

; [ > Player | Crate ] -> [ > Player | > Crate ] (When ((?level wants ((player ?here) ?dir)) (?level next (?here ?dir ?there)) (?level thing (crate ?there))) do (Claim ?level 'wants (list (list 'crate ?there) ?dir)))

A key press makes every player on the level want to move. During that tick the rules spread wants to other things, as often as they can. The next tick the level hands every want to the movement page, which moves whatever has a clear path, until nothing else can move. Datalog cannot add 1 to a coordinate, so the geometry page claims which cell is next to which, around everything that wants to move. The win page checks whether every target has a crate on it.

Swap the push page for the pull page and crates follow you around instead of being pushed. Take the movement page off the table and players still want to move, but nothing ever does.
